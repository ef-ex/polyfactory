# Fix Foldover — inverted faces after a bevel or offset

**Status:** built 2026-09-16 on branch `pf-edge-damage`; 5 mutation-paired checks green
(`tests/hda/run_fix_foldover_checks.py`); measured on the floor tile that motivated it. Not
independently audited — *implemented, self-checked*.
**This file owns:** `pf_fix_foldover` — what a foldover is, how it is found, the two repairs,
and the blind spot. Born while Hannes modelled a floor tile with `pf_edge_damage` and PolyBevel
([`edge_damage.md`](edge_damage.md)); it is its own helper tool, not part of that one.

## 1. The defect

A PolyBevel (or any offset) whose loops cross at a corner leaves a few tiny faces **mirrored**:
all their corners went past their counterparts, so the face's normal points the wrong way and
the silhouette carries a small fold. On the tile: 5 such faces (prims 247, 254, 321, 458, 614;
one with an edge 7 µm long). Hannes' 411 was a 4 µm² sliver, not inverted.

**The native tools miss it.** PolyBevel's *Stop Loops* does not prevent the crossing, and
PolyDoctor's *Correct Winding to Majority in Manifold Patch* does not fire — the fold makes the
patch non-manifold exactly there. Both tried on the tile, both left the 5 faces.

**The term:** *foldover* (an inverted element after an offset). "Small overlaps" is what it
looks like; the fix is different from an overlap's.

## 2. The tool

One input. Prim group **`pf_foldover`** on the output, and a *Fix* menu:

| Fix | does | leaves |
|---|---|---|
| Detect Only | marks the group, moves nothing | the fold, for a human to look at |
| Reverse Winding | `reverse` on the group | facing right, but the fold is still in the silhouette |
| Collapse and Fuse *(default)* | every point of an inverted face goes to the mean centre of the inverted faces it is on; a `fuse` at *Snap Distance* (1e-5) merges them and drops the collapsed faces | no fold, mesh closed, facing right |

**Detection**, one prim wrangle: a face is inverted when its normal opposes the *average* normal
of every face sharing a point with it (`dot < 0`). No thresholds, no prim numbers.

**Why no Clean.** The first repair chain ended in a `clean`; its *Degenerancy Tolerance*
(0.001) deleted legitimate tiny bevel faces and opened 24 edges on the tile. `fuse::2.0` removes
the faces the collapse degenerated by itself. Measured on the tile: collapse + fuse → 660 of 668
prims, **0 inverted, 0 open edges**; with Clean → 650 prims, 24 open edges; blast + PolyFill →
7 new inverted faces (the fill comes out facing wrong).

## 3. Checks, and what they cannot see

Fixture: a 4-division polymesh box with the two **diagonal** points of one top-face quad
swapped, which mirrors exactly that quad (prim 22). Probed on the way: swapping two *adjacent*
points, or dragging one point past its neighbour, makes **bowties**, whose Newell normal cancels
to ~0 and never reads as "opposed" — a bowtie is a different defect and this tool does not
claim it. Five checks, each with a mutation seen red: a clean box has no foldover (group forced
on); detect finds exactly the mirrored face, against an independent Newell reading (sign
flipped); Detect Only moves nothing (detect made to move a point); Reverse fixes facing and
keeps every point (group unwired); Collapse removes the fold, stays closed, drops points, leaks
no `_c` (fuse bypassed).

Blind spot, by construction: a face **meant** to oppose its neighbours — the inside of a very
sharp crease — is flagged too. Detect Only is there so a human sees the group before anything
moves.
